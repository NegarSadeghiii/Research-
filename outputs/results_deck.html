1

Survival-aware scheduling protects the sickest

Under FIFO the high-risk tier waits 9.2 days and the low-risk tier 7.9 — risk is ignored. Adaptive MPC inverts it: high risk drops to 0.4 days while low risk absorbs 23.4. Expected high-risk patients lost falls 6.12 → 4.24.

2

It reaches almost all of what is achievable

Against a perfect-information lower bound of 4.18 expected high-risk losses, adaptive MPC closes 97% of the FIFO-to-bound gap. A schedule fixed up front closes 51%.

results/mpc/expD_aggregates_N200.json · means.{fifo, static_survival, adaptive_mpc, best_achievable}.expected_lost_H

3

The value grows with congestion

At low load the policies are nearly indistinguishable — 4.51 vs 4.25 high-risk lost. At an offered load of 1.5 the gap widens to 4.36 patients (8.79 vs 4.43): a 17× larger separation.

results/mpc/expB_offered_load_N200.csv · rows target_load = 0.7 and 1.5

4

The value grows with manufacturing failure

With no failures, re-optimising buys nothing — both lose 3.85. At a 30% failure rate adaptive MPC loses 6.51 against 11.62 for a fixed schedule, a gap of 5.10 patients and 14× the gap at 5%.

results/mpc/expE_failure_rate_N200.csv · rows fail_rate = 0.00, 0.05, 0.30

5

The gain is operational, not architectural

The optimal network is m1+m3, 14 slots, at every value of life from $1 to $2M — the entire policy-relevant range. It changes only at $5M, roughly ten times any accepted reimbursement threshold. Design is invariant; scheduling is where the gain is.

results/mpc/expC_value_of_life_N200.csv · columns alpha, opened, capacity (all 8 rows)

6

A solver-free rule captures the whole benefit

The closed-form survival index and the full look-ahead MILP are indistinguishable: 4.243 expected high-risk losses each, identical cost, both closing 97%. The deployable rule needs no solver at the bedside.
